Chair modification postures — for members who have filled out the 3-month or regular membership form, received introductory training, and needed seated modifications. Sent as an attachment at onboarding when chair modification is flagged.
Note: Original document includes images for each posture. The text descriptions below are the instructor reference. For the member-facing PDF with images, attach the original document file.
Posture 1
Seated with knees bent, feet on the floor, shoulder-width apart. Relax body back with arms on top of thighs, palms upward.
Posture 2
Sit up. Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin to lengthen the back of the neck. Feet on the floor, knees bent, shoulder-width apart. Hands rest on sides of body between waist and hip area — thumbs behind, index fingers spread apart in front. Relax completely.
Posture 3
Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin. Men: cross left ankle above right knee or ankle. Women: cross right ankle above left knee or ankle. Rest hands on the ankle above the knee area, or above the knee if ankles are crossed. Shoulders relaxed.
Posture 4
Switch legs from Posture 3.
Posture 5
Sit up. Straighten and align head, neck and spine. Tilt head back 15–30 degrees. Make a loose fist with the right hand and rest it at Indang (between the eyebrows). Make a loose fist with the left hand and place it on top of the right fist — elbows open wide. Shoulders relaxed. Eyes soft. Feet on the floor, shoulder-width apart, knees bent.
Posture 6
Relax arms down. Take a rest. Keep legs the same: feet on the floor, knees bent, shoulder-width. Relax shoulders. Bring palms toward face, elbows bent, arms and hands parallel — align finger base with eyes. Maintain a one-and-a-half fist distance between your hands, and between your hands and your face. Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin. Relax shoulders.
Posture 7
Same leg position: knees bent, feet on the floor, shoulder-width. Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin. Lean forward from the pelvis at a 45-degree angle (or until you feel lower belly muscles engage); lightly grasp your knees with your hands. Relax shoulders.
Posture 8
Slowly rise back up. Arch low back and open chest. Use arms on the edge of the chair to help arch the lower back (bringing Lower Danjeon forward). Raise arms up, wrap left hand around right wrist, and relax shoulders. Gaze upward at a 15-degree angle; keep chin gently tucked. If tired, shake out arms and hands as needed and reposition.
Posture 9
Knees bent, feet on the floor, slightly wider than shoulder-width apart. Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin. Slowly bend at the hip/pelvis, releasing torso downward. Cross arms and hands and rest elbows above the knees or thighs. Check that your breath is moving — if not, come back up a little. Feel the lower belly muscles. Keep head and neck in line with the spine.
Posture 10
Slowly straighten and relax. Knees bent, feet on the floor. Place hands on thighs, close to the hip crease, with fingertips centered as if drawing them inward. Straighten and align head, neck and spine; gently tuck chin. Relax shoulders.
Posture 11
Knees bent, feet on the floor, legs slightly wider with feet angled outward slightly. Straighten and align head, neck and spine. Touch right and left index fingers and thumbs together. Position hands in front of the lower Danjeon, parallel to the floor, one fist-width away from the body. Relax shoulders. Tilt head lightly forward so gaze focuses on the circle formed by your hands. Close eyes and focus on the lower Danjeon.
Posture 12
Straighten torso and lengthen the spine as you lean back slightly, arching the low back. Gently tuck chin with eyes softly looking down toward the lower belly. Position hands lightly around the knees. Relax shoulders.

Daemaekunki 대맥운기 · Level 3
Bringing about Johwa in the Eum and Yang of the body
⬇ Download Sheet
Shimbeop: I move Ki (energy) along the path of the lower Danjeon Yeouiju Daemaek.
Recite mentally 3 times at the start of Breathing Practice, then focus consciousness on the Danjeon centered at the Seokmun.
Key Teaching Points
Daemaek — belt meridianForms a circle around the waist. Three bands, one at each Danjeon: lower (Seokmun), middle (Okdang/Cv18), upper (Indang).
Ki blockageJinki does not circulate the Daemaek instantly. Sensations of pressure or trembling indicate blockage. Increase frequency and duration of practice to clear it.
Two types of KiJinki (genuine Ki, unconscious) and Saengki (life Ki, conscious). Goal is to create Johwa — harmonious flow of both.
PostureChair version available — upper body and arm positions only. Stand, sit on chair, or sit on bed. Focus on lower Danjeon throughout.
AttitudeCalm mind and body throughout — including transitions between postures. Be kind to yourself working up to 2 minutes per posture.
Instructor Notes
Timing2 minutes per posture · 24 minutes total. Video available as guided practice.
VideoLevel 3 YouTube — chair and full versions. Follows 24-minute format. Link: https://youtu.be/[Level-3-link]

3 postures on video. Guide participants through each in sequence before finding the Seokmun point and beginning the main breathing practice.
Posture 1 — Full Body Stretch
Lie down on back, feet and toes touching.
Stretch the full body from fingertips all the way down to toes. As if doing a full-body stretch.
Back arched enough off the ground to allow a fist underneath.
Grab right wrist with left hand and bend so the palm can fully open.
Posture 2 — Legs at 90°
Bend legs at a 90-degree angle, feet shoulder-width apart.
Knees pointing upward — do not let them splay outward.
Place hands on sides, thumb and index finger spread apart.
Relax the body completely. Relax shoulders and entire body. Avoid any tension.
Posture 3 — Hands on Upper Belly
Lie on back, extend legs shoulder-width apart.
Rub hands together — make them warm.
Place warmed hands just below the rib cage in the upper belly / diaphragm area.
Relax fully and breathe naturally.
After the 3 Postures
"Any questions about the practice?" Answer Haeng Gong questions. Refer study questions to Marin / Paul.
Salt Pouch Show how the member can find where their breathing is happening — place the salt pouch there and slowly move it downward as the breath opens and relaxes downward. See full salt pouch process for details.
Must be lying down for accurate self-location. Teach this before the breathing practice begins.
Settling In
"Lie down, be comfortable."
"Relax the mind and body."
"Spread your feet shoulder-width apart, keep them relaxed."

Self-Location — Step by Step
Step 1 — Index finger in the navel Place the index finger of one hand in the navel.
Step 2 — Two fingers of the other hand Take the index and third finger of the other hand. Align the second knuckle with the navel — right next to the index finger already there.
Step 3 — Move the navel finger Remove the index finger from the navel and place it on the other side of the two fingers, centered in line with the navel.
Step 4 — This is the Seokmun point The finger now resting between the two reference fingers marks the Seokmun point.
Step 5 — Check alignment "Lift your head and check that the finger is balanced and centered left to right with the navel. Adjust if necessary."
Step 6 — Hold during practice "Keep the finger here during the breathing practice. Support the elbow with a yoga block or folded towel to relieve shoulder tension."
Why This Matters
"It is important to precisely position the lower Danjeon centered at the Seokmun — allowing for smooth lower abdominal breathing centered at the Seokmun."
"Seokmun is where Jinki — Genuine Ki — is generated and accumulated."
